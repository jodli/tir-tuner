# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Delegated: static HTML/CSS/JS on GitHub Pages, with the Rust simulator compiled to WebAssembly for anything interactive. Chosen because the medical code already lives in Rust and is verified there (sources lint, mutants, Kani), so the browser should run that code, not a JS copy of it. The existing patient pages in `docs/patient` are a Jekyll site and can stay Jekyll or move to plain static HTML. The simulation engine is `tir-tuner-sim`, which has no DuckDB dependency, so it can compile to WebAssembly.

## Users

1. People the author tells about the project: friends, colleagues, other people with type 1 diabetes who hear about it and open a link, mostly on a phone. They want to see what it is and understand it quickly.
2. People living with type 1 diabetes who want to understand their own body and pump: how insulin and carbs act, what changes absorption, what the pump settings mean.

The author is a type 1 patient on a YpsoPump with CamAPS FX and a FreeStyle Libre 3. The tool itself (analysis and setting recommendations) is for the author's own use and is not the subject of the public surfaces.

## Product Purpose

tir-tuner reads the author's Glooko exports and suggests changes to the pump settings (carb ratio, correction factor, targets) so the real CamAPS FX algorithm reaches more time in range. A paper-aligned simulator (virtual patient, sensor, controller) tests a proposed change before it goes into the pump.

The public surfaces explain the illness, not the tool: how insulin and carbohydrates move through the body, at what rate, and what changes that. Success means a patient or a curious friend understands why a setting change moves their glucose curve.

## Positioning

The explanations and any interactive demo run on the same simulator code that is checked against the published papers: every medical constant is tagged to a source and gated by a lint, mutation testing and Kani proofs. The pictures are the model's actual output, not illustrations.

## Operating Context

- Patient pages: `docs/patient/` (English) and `docs/patient/de/` (German), eight pages: index, body, food, insulin, dosing, brain (Auto mode), sensor, math-and-metrics. Mermaid diagrams.
- Developer-facing verification report: `docs/verification_report.html`, generated by `tools/gen_verification_report.py`. Not a public showcase surface.
- Planned showcase idea, not built: a "your day, two ways" page where a slider changes a setting (for example the breakfast carb ratio) and the simulated day and time in range update live.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Every public page ships in English and German. German pages use "Sie".
- No real patient data on public pages. Only the virtual patient. The author's Glooko data and `settings.toml` stay local and gitignored.
- Papers and the claim registers stay local (copyrighted). Public pages carry no paper quotes.
- Patient pages contain no formulas, paper citations, crate names or tool talk. Plain language.
- Must work on phones.
- Terminology: time in range (TIR, 70–180 mg/dL), time below range (TBR), carb ratio, correction factor, target, insulin on board, Auto mode, Boost, Ease-off. Both mg/dL and mmol/L appear (conversion 18.0156).
- Open: whether and how fast one simulated day runs in WebAssembly; the controller re-plans every 15 minutes.

## Brand Commitments

Writing voice: plain, specific, no em-dashes, no marketing prose. The current site title is "The artificial pancreas project, for patients". No logo or visual identity exists yet.

## Evidence on Hand

- Simulator results on the virtual patient (for example TIR and TBR over seed sets) can be generated from the code.
- The CamAPS FX user manual and papers are local only.
- No testimonials, users, clinical results or endorsements exist. Do not fabricate any.

## Product Principles

1. Explain the illness, not the tool.
2. Every number and curve comes from the verified simulator or a cited source; nothing invented for effect.
3. Not medical advice: nothing replaces the diabetes team or the pump manual, and every surface says so.
4. Public means virtual: real data never leaves the author's machine.
5. Plain language in both languages, readable on a phone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No standard was set. Inferred, not confirmed: charts need text equivalents and must not rely on color alone for in range, low and high.
